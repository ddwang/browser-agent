## Failures and judging limits

- **Amazon--10:** the actor substituted a sporting-goods protection plan for an
  unverified PS4 plan. The trace does not establish product eligibility. The
  question also leaves the coverage bracket unspecified; that ambiguity does not
  justify the unsupported substitution.
- **Google Map--16:** the actor selected a charging result without establishing
  that it was closest, and asserted a distance without checking it. The question
  does not identify which Smithsonian museum, a limitation recorded before its
  outcome. It remains a failure in the denominator.
- **ESPN--8:** likely temporal judging error. The saved screenshot selects
  `2025-26 Regular Season` and displays the three players/teams reported by the
  actor. The judge rejected it because an upcoming October scoreboard supposedly
  meant that season had not ended. However, this run occurred on September 15,
  2026; the NBA scheduled the season's end for April 12, 2026.
  [Official NBA calendar](https://pr.nba.com/2025-26-nba-regular-season-schedule/).
  This supports a likely false negative, not a silently adjusted score: the
  original NOT SUCCESS remains, and no second judge was called.

Favorable verdicts also need scrutiny: for Amazon--35, the judge used capacity for
6–8 cards as evidence of at least six card slots. Capacity alone does not establish
the number of distinct slots. This is a potential verification gap, not a revised
verdict; no adjusted success rate is reported in either direction.

The calendar check happened only after the complete run, to audit the rationale;
it did not feed the actor or alter the candidate, rubric, or result. The saved
actor memory includes the run date, but the judge does not explicitly receive a
reference date: `AgentMemory.loadJSON` restores observations, not saved instructions.
Do not blindly copy actor instructions into a judge. A future evaluation design
should provide trusted run metadata separately and validate temporal judgments
with fresh, independent cases.

Question-only ambiguity notes were recorded before the affected ESPN/Maps outcomes.
They also noted the unspecified GP-percentage denominator, the unspecified season,
and the changing nature of prices/headlines/maps data. Both favorable and unfavorable
verdicts may contain judgment error; this was not a complete blinded human rescore.

## Precautions against overfitting

The original smoke, scroll, and hard suites are explicitly development data. The
holdout's seed, catalog checksum, and selected-content checksum were frozen in
`e81b521`, before native-output implementation. Selection used hashed ordering
without inspecting chosen question text or expected success, plus a fixed
purchase/account-write exclusion filter. Four tasks each came from Amazon, ESPN,
and Google Map—sites absent from all inspected development suites.

The candidate was committed and verified before its questions were opened for a
read-only safety check. No source changes occurred during the holdout, and no
behavior changes followed its outcomes. CLI guards require deliberate, complete,
clean-candidate holdout exposure and reject development selection of reserved sites,
selective reruns, replacement, and separate rejudging.

No site names, expected answers, special filenames, selectors, task IDs, or
case-specific browsing hints were added to agent logic. The fixes used synthetic
protocol tests, randomized data, real loopback browser fixtures, and model-client
fixtures rather than reminders matching failed task answers. Public WebVoyager
tasks may exist in model pretraining; this controls development exposure, not
pretraining contamination. Twelve tasks are a small sample and not a hard-suite
holdout equivalent.
